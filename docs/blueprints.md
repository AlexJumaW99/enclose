# Blueprints

**Blueprint** (B) puts the model's drawings where the view was. B again or the button
brings the view back, as it was left but with nothing picked out. Esc does too, once it
has put a model that is pulled apart back together, and stepped back out of whatever
was gone into since.

## The sheets

The model is drawn flat, from four of its corners and then from each of its six sides,
each on a sheet of its own, upright and four wide to five high, the shape of an
Instagram feed post. A corner shows three sides at once, all turned equally from the eye (an
isometric drawing): the top or the bottom, with the front and the left side or the front
and the right.

The sheets stand in a row that is gone along sideways, and a wheel that only turns up
and down goes along it too. So does the pointer: press anywhere on the row and pull it
along. There is nothing on a sheet but the drawing, in the middle of it: no border and
no title block.

Click a sheet and it has the view to itself: the whole view is its paper, and the
drawing is done again to fill it, to whatever scale that comes to, with its lines as
thin and its writing as small as on any sheet. (What is saved is the drawing on
its own paper, to its own scale, which looks much the same.) Everything else that is over the view is put away meanwhile: the
project's card, the buttons down the right, the settings. The switches at the foot of
the sheets are still there, to change what the sheet shows, and at the top right is a
button that puts it back in the row. Esc does that too (after it has put the model
together, if it is pulled apart), and the left and right arrow keys go to the sheet
before it and the one after.

## The lines

The lines are the model's edges and the outlines of its curved faces, with whatever is
behind something left out. That is worked out from the model and not from a picture of
it, so a line stops exactly where it goes behind another part, however closely the
drawing is looked at afterwards. The lines are a point and a half wide.

A drawing is on paper of its own, no bigger than it needs: the drawing, with 15 mm
clear all round it, and room at either side for the names of the parts where it has
those. It is made as big as fits paper the size of A2 on its side, to a scale drawings
are made to: life size, or the least reduction that fits (1:2, 1:5, 1:10 and so on),
or of a small thing the most enlargement that does (2:1, 5:1, 10:1). The six sides are
all to the one scale, and the four corners, which take more room, to one of their own.
So the model is most of what is on the paper, and what is written round it is small
beside it.

A sheet in the row is that drawing seen small, all of it, with what is written on it
as small as that makes it. It is there to be looked at, and is not set out again to be
read at that size: click it to read it.

## What is drawn

What is drawn is what the view had on show: pick parts in the list and the sheets are of
those, put some out of sight with H and they are left out, pull the model apart and it
is drawn pulled apart.

The cut and the ground are the view's, and their buttons wait until it is back. So do
the cards of the other projects, which close up out of the sheets' way and leave the
open project's card at the top.

## Exploded, dimensions and labels

At the foot of the sheets are three switches.

**Exploded** draws the model pulled apart, as E does, and brings up beside it the slider
that pulls it further.

**Dimensions** has the sheets say how big the model is: its width, height and depth,
each as a thin line with an arrowhead at either end and the figure over it. A side shows
the two it can, across its top and down its left, and a corner all three. They are of
what is drawn, so of the parts on show, and are saved with a sheet, in a group of their
own. Beside the switch, while it is on, is what they are in: millimetres or inches,
which the settings have too (General, Blueprints).

**Labels** has the sheets say which part is which, the way a diagram in a manual does:
the name of each part in a column at either side of the drawing, with a line of dots
from it that ends in a bigger dot on the part. A part is named on a sheet where any
line of it shows there, so a model that is put together has only what is on its outside
named. Pulled apart into its pieces (1x, see [Explode](section-explode-grid.md#explode))
it is the pieces that are named, each group once and not the parts of it; pulled apart
again, into its parts (2x), every part that is in sight. The names take room: the paper
keeps as much of its width clear of the drawing as the longest of them needs, and where
the drawing no longer fits in what is left, the drawings are made to the next scale down.
A name too long for its column is cut short, and where there are more parts than a
drawing's paper has room to name, about two hundred, the smallest go unnamed. The names are saved
with a sheet, in a group of their own.

## Saving

The arrow over a sheet saves its drawing as an SVG, the size of the drawing's own paper
and in millimetres, to the scale it is drawn to, with nothing in it but the lines: those of each part together under the part's name, and
the parts of each group under the group's, so that a program that draws (Illustrator,
Figma, Inkscape) opens it with the model's own layers. Its tooltip says the scale.

**Download all SVG**, after the switches, saves every sheet at once, in a folder named
after the model inside one you choose, and opens that folder once they are saved.
